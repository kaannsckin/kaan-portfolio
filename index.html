Graduation projectGrade AA

Optimizing campaign management for resident tutors.

For Kunduz, our team studied how to match fluctuating hourly follow up demand with tutor supply. The practical tension was clear: maintain a high fulfillment rate without letting campaign cost drift out of control.

01 / Forecast

Demand before the rush

The XGBoost model used recent hourly demand, rolling statistics, time features and special hour signals. On the test set it reached an R² of 0.8475, with MAE 11.05.

02 / Operations

Supply as a queue

An M/M/c/c model translated active tutor capacity into an expected fulfillment rate. This made the relationship between price, tutor participation and service level explicit.

03 / Decision

A price for the next hour

The optimization balanced campaign spend with the penalty of unmet demand while respecting budget, nonnegativity and target fulfillment constraints.

Game design case · University years

Analysis was the entry point. Solve Stop was the outcome.

My interest in the game industry led me to work through several cases during university. For this one, I played and analyzed the first 50 levels of Royal Match and Toon Blast, looking at mechanics, difficulty curves, level design, power ups, interface and retention systems. Then I used those observations to design my own tap to blast game.

The original mechanic Matched items do not simply disappear. They upgrade into higher tier tools, then into vehicles. The cell a player taps matters, so a familiar action creates a more strategic board.

Stars for Starbucks · Two projects

One forecast for operations.
One game for the wait.

Project 01 / Filter coffee

Forecast, then brew deliberately.

The model translated store level demand into a brewing schedule for two urns, accounting for quarter, half and full batches and a one hour product life.

  • Historical demand forecast with special day adjustment
  • Past, current and next hour machine schedule
  • Live demand and inventory dashboard concept
  • Designed to reduce waste and support margin
Project 02 / Queue experience

Make the waiting time useful.

Stars Game of the Day invited mobile order customers to play a short daily game while their drink was prepared, turning idle waiting into a light reward loop.

  • Daily game inside the mobile order journey
  • Stars as a participation reward
  • Reward economics tested against customer frequency
  • Goal: stronger waiting satisfaction and app use
